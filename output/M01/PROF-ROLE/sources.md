# Source Pack: PROF-ROLE

## Basis

This source pack uses author course materials and common systems-analysis practice. External research is disabled for this Topic Passport.

## Core Teaching Position

A system analyst helps a team turn a vague need into an agreed, testable description of how a system should behave. The analyst reduces uncertainty: clarifies goals, users, process, data, rules, exceptions, integrations, and acceptance criteria.

## Key Ideas

- A system is not only an application screen. It includes people, process, data, business rules, permissions, integrations, logs, errors, and support routines.
- A system analyst is not a passive note-taker. The role asks clarifying questions, finds contradictions, makes hidden rules explicit, and helps people agree.
- The analyst is not the project manager, developer, QA, UX designer, product owner, or business owner. The analyst works with all of them and prepares shared understanding for implementation and verification.
- For beginners, the first useful mental pattern is: who participates, what goal they have, what data is needed, what rules apply, what the system does, what happens on errors, and how we know the result is correct.

## Example Pool

### Compliance
A company wants to check new clients before activation. The analyst clarifies when the check starts, what client data is used, what statuses exist, what happens on a match, who can override the result, what must be logged, and who receives notifications.

### Fintech
A payment service wants daily transfer limits. The analyst clarifies which operations count toward the limit, whether limits depend on client type, when the check happens, and what message the user sees when the limit is exceeded.

### Edtech
An online school wants automatic certificates. The analyst clarifies completion criteria, retakes, certificate fields, delivery channels, and what happens when a student changes their name.

### Marketplace
A marketplace wants product returns from the personal account. The analyst clarifies return windows, product categories, reasons, statuses, integrations with logistics and payments, and notifications for buyer and seller.

### Internal HR Tool
A company wants vacation approval in an internal portal. The analyst clarifies employee, manager, and HR roles; balance checks; approval steps; calendar integration; and cancellation rules.

## Review Caveats

The lecture must not overload beginners with BPMN, UML, API design, SQL, microservices, or legal details. Those topics should appear only as future areas or as lightly explained examples.
